How the math works

Points per $1
points ÷ face value
Each card has a point price. $100 of LEGO costs 9,250 points; $100 of Amazon costs 10,315. That difference is the whole story.
Cash back
100 × earn rate ÷ points per $1
In a 5× category you earn 5 points per $1 spent; everywhere else, 1. Spend $100, redeem the points, and the card's price decides your real return.
Versus 1¢/point
100 ÷ points per $1 × 100
The common baseline is 100 points ≈ $1. Above 100% the gift card beats that baseline; below it, you are giving value away.
Card size
$25 spread ≈ 0.2–0.4 points worse per $1
Every $25 card in this catalogue is worse per point than the same store's $50 or $100 card. Buy the largest denomination you will genuinely spend.

A gift card is only worth its face value if you were going to spend there anyway. A 5.4% return at a store you visit twice a year is worse than 4.6% at the shop you use every week — and worse than keeping the points for something you actually need. Use the ranking as a shortlist, not a shopping list.

What this page does not know

  • Point prices are from one catalogue snapshot (November 2026) and can change without notice.
  • It assumes no promotional multipliers, no birthday bonus and no cap. Bonuses scale every rate here up or down equally.
  • Only e-gift cards are covered. Travel, statement credits and merchandise redemptions are priced differently.
  • Gift cards carry the usual risk: they can be lost, expire under the issuer's terms, and tie your money to one retailer.

How to use it in two minutes

  • Cross off every spending type you don't use.
  • Leave “Hide $25 cards” on — they are consistently the worst value.
  • Sort by cash back and take the top card at a store you'd visit anyway.
  • Check the card's own points price in your rewards portal before redeeming; it is the only number that matters.
